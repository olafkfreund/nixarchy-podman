# Intent: The menu ignores the screen it is drawn on

## Problem

The owner reports that the text and the windows do not follow the desktop's
size and scale. They do not, and the reason is narrower than it looks.

Omarchy already provides the scaling system: `Style.fontScale` from the
theme's `fontBaseSize`, `Style.effectiveSpacingScale`, `Style.space()` and
the `Style.font.*` tokens. This plugin uses them properly — every font size,
width, margin, padding and column width in all six QML files routes through a
token, with no literal pixel bypass anywhere. Change the theme's base size and
the plugin follows. That part is right and should not be touched.

What is wrong is that two constants sit on top of that system and one of them
is applied the wrong way.

**The 1.45 factor is a render transform, so nothing reflows.** `Menu.qml:36`
declares `uiScale: 1.45`; `Menu.qml:154-156` lays the view out at its natural
size, divides the frame back down, and then draws it 1.45 times larger with
`scale:` and `transformOrigin: Item.TopLeft`. Because the magnification
happens after layout, wrapping and eliding are computed at the small size and
the result is stretched. A label that would fit at the real size still elides;
a wrap lands where the small layout put it. This is a layout defect
independent of how crisply the glyphs rasterise — and that rasterisation
question is genuinely open, because Qt Quick's distance-field text scales
cleanly and nobody has checked it on the hardware.

The fleet has already settled this. Both `nixarchy-pkg`
(`/nix/store/2fn1qcqvxv7z95n568w41nbn87m55jzz-nixarchy-pkg/Menu.qml:35-36`,
`Card.qml:19,37`, `OptionForm.qml:47-48`) and `nixarchy-flatsnap`
(`/nix/store/3bd1wdza1v1m8l6zgjc733frcm6csx06-nixarchy-flatsnap-plugin/Menu.qml:22-23`)
use the same 1.45 factor as a layout-time multiplier — `textScale` with
`function px(base) { return Math.round(base * root.textScale) }` — and
`grep -c 'scale:'` returns 0 for every `.qml` file in both. Verified directly.
`nixarchy-distrobox` and `nixarchy-microvm` still carry the transform, so this
plugin is on the wrong side of a split the fleet has already resolved.

**Neither factor consults the screen.** `uiScale` is 1.45 on a 1080p laptop
panel and 1.45 on a 4K monitor. `Menu.qml:37` pins `viewWidth` at
`Style.space(680)`, capped only at 90% of the panel width, so on a
3840-wide display the card is roughly 986 logical px adrift in the middle of
the screen and never grows into the room. `Menu.qml:162` does derive the list
height from `panel.height * 0.85` — and that value is genuinely consumed,
through `PodmanView.qml:373` to `ResourceList.qml:89` — but it wraps the
result in `Math.min(Style.space(560), …)`, so past a certain screen height the
screen-derived figure is thrown away by a constant. `Screen.devicePixelRatio`
appears nowhere in the plugin.

`manifest.json` offers no size or scale setting, so none of this is
adjustable by the person looking at it.

What is *not* wrong, and should stay: the menu resolves the focused monitor on
every `open()` (`Menu.qml:39-46,71,100`) and re-derives geometry from live
`panel.width`/`panel.height` bindings rather than capturing them once, despite
being `keepLoaded`.

## Proposed outcome

- The menu's type and geometry are produced by layout at the intended size, so
  wrapping and eliding are computed against the size the user actually sees.
- The card grows with the screen it opens on instead of sitting at a fixed
  width, and a tall screen yields a taller list.
- This plugin uses the same mechanism as `nixarchy-pkg` and
  `nixarchy-flatsnap`, so the fleet has one answer rather than three.
- The owner's report is resolved on their own monitors, checked on the
  hardware rather than reasoned about.

## Affected users and systems

- `Menu.qml` chiefly; `PodmanView.qml` and `ResourceList.qml` if the size has
  to be threaded rather than transformed.
- The full-screen menu only. `Panel.qml`'s popup anchors under the bar button
  and is not in scope.
- Anyone on a monitor that is not the one this was tuned on — and anyone with
  two monitors of different scales.
- `docs/img`: a geometry change invalidates the menu captures. The budget is
  at 69% of 8 MB, and AGENTS.md requires retaking what a change makes wrong.

## Constraints

- Do not touch `Style.font.*` or `Style.space()` usage. They are correct, they
  are shared shell-wide, and changing them means patching `qs.Commons`, which
  is outside this repository.
- The menu is `keepLoaded`: anything derived from the screen must be
  re-derived on `open()`, never captured at load.
- The menu must keep opening on the focused monitor.
- No hardcoded colours, no new dependency, and the result must stay readable
  on the smallest screen it currently works on — growing into a large display
  must not break the small one.
- Retake the affected captures in the same PR, within the 8 MB budget.

## Measured on the owner's hardware

Taken before drafting the questions below, because it removes some of them.

```
DP-1     2560x1440@143  scale=1
DP-2     2560x1440@143  scale=1  (focused)
HDMI-A-1 1920x1080@60   scale=1
```

Every monitor is `scale=1`, so `devicePixelRatio` is 1 on all three and Qt's
HiDPI layer is doing nothing. No `base-size` is set anywhere in
`~/.config/omarchy/`, so `Style.fontBaseSize` is its default 12 and
`spacingScale` is 1 — `Style.space(px) === px` exactly, `Style.font.body`
is 12.

So there is no desktop scale here for anything to follow, and the DPR
question this intent would otherwise have opened with is moot. Two things
follow.

**Small text is a theme setting before it is a code change.** At
`fontBaseSize: 12` and DPR 1, body text renders at 12 physical pixels on a
27" 1440p panel. Omarchy's own answer is the theme's `[font] base-size`,
which drives `Style.fontScale` and `Style.effectiveSpacingScale` and so
rescales the whole shell consistently. That is one line of user config and no
code, and it may be the entire complaint. It should be tried first.

**Deriving `uiScale` from `devicePixelRatio` is wrong twice over.** It was
proposed as `Math.max(1.0, Math.min(1.6, targetScreen.devicePixelRatio))`.
On this hardware that is 1.0 on all three monitors — smaller than today's
1.45, the opposite of the report. It is also wrong in principle:
`ShellScreen.width` is logical with DPR already divided out, and Qt applies
DPR at rasterisation, so a layout factor multiplied by DPR applies it twice.
`uiScale` encodes viewing distance, not pixel density.

What remains is real and unaffected by any of the above: the transform does
not reflow, and the constants do not adapt across the owner's own monitors —
the card is ~986px on all three, 51% of the 1920-wide screen and 38.5% of the
2560-wide ones, while `Menu.qml:162` caps the list at 560 where
`panel.height * 0.85` offers 1224. The input that actually varies here is
logical screen size, not DPR.

The blurriness hypothesis is also dead: there is no `renderType` override in
the plugin, and the two `qs.Ui` components that set `Text.NativeRendering`
(`WidgetButton.qml`, `OpticalGlyph.qml`) are not imported, so every `Text`
uses distance-field `Text.QtRendering` and scales cleanly. Remove the
transform for the reflow, not for the rasterisation.

## Open questions

1. **Does raising the theme's `[font] base-size` resolve the complaint?** If
   it does, the scope here shrinks to the reflow and the non-adaptive
   constants, and the "text is too small" half disappears entirely. This is
   the first thing to try and costs one config line.
2. **Match the fleet exactly, or improve on it?** `textScale` + `px()` is
   verified in `nixarchy-pkg` and `nixarchy-flatsnap` and fixes the reflow,
   but it keeps 1.45 as a constant and so leaves the screen-blindness.
   Adopting it and making the factor vary diverges from them — possibly for
   the better, and possibly something to offer back.
3. **What should the factor derive from?** Logical screen width or height
   against a reference, clamped — not `devicePixelRatio`, for the reasons
   above. The owner should say what looks right on the 1920 and the 2560
   panels, since this is taste as much as arithmetic.
4. **Is `viewWidth`'s fixed 680 worth keeping** as a proportion-preserving
   base once the factor varies, or should the width derive from the screen
   directly and the cap at `panel.width * 0.9` do the rest?
